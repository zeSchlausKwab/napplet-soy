# Background music direction

Paste this into ElevenLabs Music. Request a 30-second instrumental; the cue times
below describe the desired visual arc, not a guarantee of frame-accurate generation.
The current film uses the locally synthesized score until a generated track is chosen.

> Create a 30-second instrumental for a playful indie video-game film. Modern
> chiptune meets warm electronic adventure music, 128 BPM, 4/4. A catchy square-wave
> melody, bouncy triangle-wave bass, sparkling pixel arpeggios, crisp punchy drums
> and subtle analogue pads. Charming, mischievous and slightly rebellious; nostalgic
> arcade energy with a polished contemporary mix. Fun and confident, not childish
> or corporate. Keep the melody original.
>
> Start immediately with a small, curious melodic idea. Around 8 seconds, introduce
> a cheeky new bass riff and stronger drums as a cute platform-game character gets
> a shotgun. Around 16–18 seconds, weave that riff together with the original melody
> into a satisfying, harmonious resolution: two creators' ideas becoming one.
> From 18–24 seconds, let the combined theme feel joyful and triumphant. From
> 24–29 seconds, build a smooth ascending arpeggio and lift in energy, as if flying
> straight into the game. Land on a bright, inviting arcade-style final chord near
> 29 seconds, with a short musical tail ending at 30 seconds. It should feel like
> “your turn to play,” not an epic trailer finale.
>
> Instrumental only: no vocals, speech, gunshots, gameplay sound effects or typing.
> Avoid harsh leads, heavy distortion, dramatic trailer booms and long intros.
> Leave space for separate game sounds. Clear melody, warm bass, controlled dynamics.
